On October 22, 2026, China's National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) approved a new indication for teclistamab injection for adults with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma who have received at least one prior line of therapy, including an anti-CD38 monoclonal antibody and lenalidomide. The approval, supported by findings from the Phase 3 MajesTEC-9 study, provides a new immunotherapy option for eligible patients.

## I. Challenges in Multiple Myeloma Treatment

Multiple myeloma is a malignant plasma cell disorder that primarily affects the bone marrow. It can lead to bone damage, anemia, kidney impairment, and abnormalities in immune function.

In recent years, treatment options have expanded with the development of proteasome inhibitors, immunomodulatory drugs, and anti-CD38 monoclonal antibodies. However, disease relapse, treatment resistance, and limited options after multiple lines of therapy remain important challenges.

For patients previously treated with an anti-CD38 monoclonal antibody and lenalidomide, therapies with different mechanisms of action are particularly important. The new indication for teclistamab expands its use to an earlier stage of relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma, offering an additional treatment option for eligible adults.

## II. Teclistamab: A Bispecific Antibody Targeting BCMA and CD3

![Teclistamab binding BCMA on a multiple myeloma cell and CD3 on a T cell](/assets/images/teclistamab-bcma-cd3-mechanism-diagram.png)

Teclistamab, marketed as TECVAYLI®, is a bispecific T-cell–engaging antibody developed by Johnson & Johnson. It simultaneously targets B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA) and CD3, a molecule expressed on T cells.

BCMA is an important therapeutic target in multiple myeloma. By binding to both BCMA on myeloma cells and CD3 on T cells, teclistamab brings these cells into close proximity, activating T cells and promoting the destruction of myeloma cells.

Unlike conventional chemotherapy and some targeted therapies, this approach harnesses the patient's own T cells to generate an antitumor response. Teclistamab therefore represents an important immunotherapeutic strategy for eligible patients with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma.

## III. MajesTEC-9 Study: Key Efficacy Findings

The Phase 3 MajesTEC-9 study evaluated teclistamab against standard treatment in patients with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma who had received one to three previous lines of therapy.

The control regimens included pomalidomide, bortezomib, and dexamethasone (PVd), or carfilzomib and dexamethasone (Kd). Approximately 85% of enrolled patients were refractory to anti-CD38 monoclonal antibodies, while approximately 79% were refractory to lenalidomide.

The study reported the following key results:
- 71% lower relative risk of disease progression or death, with a hazard ratio (HR) of 0.29.
- 40% lower relative risk of death, with an HR of 0.60.
- 65.9% achieved a complete response or better, compared with 16.8% in the standard-treatment group.

At the interim analysis, the estimated 18-month progression-free survival rate was 69.8% with teclistamab, compared with 26.9% with standard treatment. The estimated 18-month overall survival rates were 79.2% and 68.6%, respectively.

These findings demonstrate clinically meaningful improvements in progression-free and overall survival in the study population. However, relative risk reductions should not be interpreted as guaranteed benefits for individual patients, and treatment outcomes may vary according to disease characteristics, prior therapies, and overall health.

## IV. Safety Profile: Balancing Efficacy and Risk

Although teclistamab activates T cells to fight cancer, its mechanism of action can also lead to serious adverse events. Safety monitoring is therefore an essential part of treatment.

In MajesTEC-9, adverse events occurred in 99.7% of patients receiving teclistamab and 97.9% of those receiving standard treatment. Grade 3–4 adverse events occurred in 84.9% and 76.3% of patients, respectively. Grade 5 adverse events were reported in 6.5% of the teclistamab group and 3.5% of the control group.

Key safety findings included:
- **Infections:** Grade 3–4 infections occurred in 41.6% of patients receiving teclistamab, compared with 29.0% in the standard-treatment group.
- **Cytokine release syndrome (CRS):** Reported in 66.0% of teclistamab-treated patients, mostly at grade 1–2.
- **Immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS):** Reported in 4.1% of patients, predominantly at grade 1–2.

These findings highlight the importance of infection prevention, close monitoring, and appropriate management of immune-related adverse events. Teclistamab should be administered under the supervision of qualified healthcare professionals in accordance with the locally approved prescribing information.
