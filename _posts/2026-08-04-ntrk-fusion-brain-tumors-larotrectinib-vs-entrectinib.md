## What Is NTRK Fusion and Why Is It Associated With Brain Tumors?

The **NTRK gene family** includes:

- **NTRK1**
- **NTRK2**
- **NTRK3**

These three genes encode **TRK (Tropomyosin Receptor Kinase)** proteins, which are involved in biological processes such as nervous system development and cellular signal transduction.

When NTRK genes undergo abnormal fusion with other genes, they can produce continuously activated TRK fusion proteins, which may affect signaling pathways related to cell proliferation and survival.

NTRK fusion is a relatively rare genetic alteration with clear molecular significance. It can occur in various solid tumors, including:

- Gliomas
- Infantile high-grade gliomas
- Glioblastoma (in a small proportion of cases)
- Other glial tumors
- Additional pediatric and adult central nervous system (CNS) tumors

Because NTRK fusions occur at a relatively low frequency, not every brain tumor patient requires NTRK testing. Testing decisions are usually made based on factors such as patient age, pathological classification, and disease stage.

## Why Is Genetic Testing Needed for NTRK Fusion Brain Tumors?

With continuous updates to the WHO classification of [central nervous system tumors](https://dengyuemed.com/product/nervous-system-diseases/), molecular biomarkers are playing an increasingly important role in brain tumor diagnosis and treatment decision-making.

Traditional brain tumor diagnosis mainly relies on imaging examinations, pathological analysis, and immunohistochemistry. However, confirmation of **NTRK fusion** requires additional molecular testing.

Common testing methods include:

### 1. RNA Sequencing

RNA sequencing can directly detect NTRK fusion transcripts and identify different fusion patterns, making it suitable for analyzing complex tumor samples.

### 2. DNA Sequencing

DNA-based next-generation sequencing (NGS) can be used to screen for NTRK-related genetic alterations, although some fusion events may be limited by the testing platform.

### 3. Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

TRK protein immunohistochemistry can be used as an initial screening method. Positive results usually require further confirmation through molecular testing.

Larotrectinib is a highly selective TRK inhibitor that primarily targets:

- NTRK1 fusions
- NTRK2 fusions
- NTRK3 fusions

Its mechanism of action involves inhibiting TRK fusion protein-related signaling pathways and reducing the impact of abnormal pathway activation on tumor cell behavior.

Larotrectinib is not limited to a single cancer type. Instead, treatment selection is based on the presence of NTRK fusion, representing one approach within the tumor-agnostic precision medicine model.

## Application of Entrectinib in NTRK Fusion Brain Tumors

![Entrectinib (Rozlytrek)](/assets/images/entrectinib-rozlytrek.png)

Entrectinib is a multi-target tyrosine kinase inhibitor that primarily acts on:

- TRKA
- TRKB
- TRKC
- ROS1
- ALK

Unlike Larotrectinib, Entrectinib targets not only NTRK fusions but also additional kinase targets.

### Key Features of Entrectinib ([Rozlytrek](https://dengyuemed.com/product/rozlytrek-entrectinib-nsclc/))

### Multi-Target Mechanism of Action

Entrectinib can act on multiple related signaling pathways and has been widely studied in tumors with different molecular backgrounds.

### Central Nervous System Application

Because brain tumor treatment involves the important challenge of the blood-brain barrier (BBB), the ability of drugs to reach the central nervous system is an important consideration.

Entrectinib has demonstrated characteristics associated with blood-brain barrier penetration and has therefore received attention in research involving NTRK fusion brain tumors and brain metastases.

Both drugs are important treatment options for NTRK fusion tumors. Selection depends on multiple factors, including:

Therefore, drug selection should be based on individualized medical assessment.
