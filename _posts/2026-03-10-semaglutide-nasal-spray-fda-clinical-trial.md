## Semaglutide: A Star Drug in the GLP-1 Field

Semaglutide is a **GLP-1 receptor agonist (GLP-1 RA)** primarily used to treat **type 2 diabetes and obesity**.

By mimicking the action of the natural **GLP-1 hormone**, the drug can:

- Stimulate insulin secretion  
- Suppress glucagon release  
- Slow gastric emptying  
- Increase feelings of fullness  

Currently, semaglutide products developed by Novo Nordisk are widely used worldwide, including:

- **Ozempic** – diabetes treatment  
- **Wegovy** – weight management  
- **Rybelsus** – oral semaglutide  

As global obesity rates continue to rise, **demand for semaglutide continues to grow rapidly**.

## Nasal Spray Formulation: A New Direction for GLP-1 Drugs

Most semaglutide products currently on the market are **injectable or oral formulations**. However, injectable treatments may present adherence challenges for some patients.

A **nasal spray formulation** may offer several potential advantages.

### 1. More Convenient Administration

Patients would not need injections or tablets.  
The medication could simply be delivered through the **nasal cavity**.

### 2. Rapid Absorption

The nasal mucosa is highly vascularized, which may allow drugs to **enter the bloodstream quickly**.

### 3. Improved Patient Compliance

For patients requiring **long-term treatment for diabetes or obesity**, non-invasive delivery methods are often easier to accept.

Because of these factors, semaglutide nasal spray is considered a **potential breakthrough in GLP-1 drug delivery technology**.

## What Does FDA Approval for Clinical Trials Mean?

Receiving **FDA approval to enter the clinical stage** typically indicates that the drug has completed **preclinical research and safety evaluations**.

The semaglutide nasal spray will now move through the standard **clinical trial process**.

### Phase I Clinical Trials
Evaluate the drug’s **safety and tolerability** in humans.

### Phase II Clinical Trials
Investigate **efficacy and optimal dosage**.

### Phase III Clinical Trials
Large-scale trials designed to confirm **safety and therapeutic effectiveness**.

If these trials are successfully completed, the drug may eventually submit a **New Drug Application (NDA)** to the FDA.
