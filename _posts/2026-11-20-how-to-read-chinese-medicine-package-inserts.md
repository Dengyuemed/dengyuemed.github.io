Have you ever purchased traditional Chinese medicine at a pharmacy in China but struggled to understand the Chinese packaging and instructions? Without being familiar with the language, it can be difficult to identify a medicine's ingredients, intended uses, and directions for use.

For international consumers who are unfamiliar with Chinese, purchasing traditional Chinese medicine in China can be a challenging experience. The wide variety of medicine packaging, different dosage forms, and unfamiliar Chinese terminology may all cause confusion.

What ingredients does a medicine contain? What is it traditionally used for? How should it be taken? Are there any precautions to consider? These are all important questions when purchasing traditional Chinese medicine.

In fact, understanding Chinese medicine labels and package inserts may be easier than you think. By learning a few common Chinese pharmaceutical terms and becoming familiar with the key sections of a package insert, even consumers who do not speak Chinese can gain a better understanding of the products they purchase.

This guide explores how to read Chinese medicine labels and package inserts, with a focus on practical information that international consumers should know when purchasing traditional Chinese medicine in China.

## Ⅰ. What Should You Look for First on a Chinese Medicine Package?

Imagine that you are visiting China and enter a local pharmacy, where you come across a traditional Chinese medicine product with attractive packaging. You may first notice its name or the images on the box, but these details alone are not enough to help you understand the product fully.

Before making a purchase, it is advisable to check the following information.

**1. Medicine Name**

The medicine name is the first step in identifying a product. Chinese patent medicine packaging generally displays the generic medicine name. Some products may also include a trade name or brand name.

Consumers should prioritize the generic name rather than relying solely on the brand or packaging to determine a medicine's intended use. If further information is needed, the generic name can also be used to search for relevant product details.

**2. Ingredients**

The ingredients section provides information about the herbal materials and other active ingredients contained in a medicine.

For example, some Chinese patent medicines may contain herbal ingredients such as Astragalus (Huangqi), Ginseng (Renshen), Licorice (Gancao), and Angelica (Danggui).

👉 This information is particularly important for people with known ingredient allergies or those who are already taking other medications.

**3. Functions and Indications**

This is a distinctive term commonly found on traditional Chinese medicine packaging. It describes a medicine's functions and intended uses within the framework of traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) theory.

For example, packaging may include terms such as "clearing heat and detoxifying," "dispelling wind and relieving exterior syndromes," or "tonifying qi and nourishing blood."

These expressions are based on traditional Chinese medicine concepts and do not necessarily correspond directly to specific diseases or diagnoses in modern medicine. Therefore, consumers should not determine whether a medicine is suitable for them based solely on these descriptions.

**4. Specifications**

Specifications generally indicate the unit content, strength, or package quantity of a medicine. Examples include:
- 24 tablets per box
- 10 g per sachet
- 100 mL per bottle

It is important to understand that package specifications are not the same as the recommended dose for each administration. The actual dosage and frequency of use should always follow the instructions provided in the corresponding package insert.

**5. Manufacturing Date and Expiry Date**

When purchasing medicine, consumers should check the manufacturing date and expiry date to avoid purchasing or using expired products.

The packaging should also be inspected for damage, moisture exposure, or other abnormalities. For medicines that require special storage conditions, consumers should ensure that the products have been stored appropriately.

These basic details help consumers identify medicines and determine whether the products are within their stated shelf life.

## Ⅱ. Understanding Chinese Medicine Package Inserts: Five Key Terms to Know

While packaging labels provide a quick overview of a medicine, the package insert contains more detailed product information.

For international consumers, the following Chinese terms are particularly useful to understand.

**1. Directions and Dosage**

This is one of the most important sections to read before using a medicine. It explains the route of administration, the amount to be used each time, and the recommended frequency.

Common expressions include:
- **Oral administration:** To be taken by mouth.
- **For external use:** To be applied externally.
- **Once daily:** To be used once a day.
- **Two tablets each time:** To take two tablets per administration.

Different dosage forms may have significantly different administration requirements. Tablets, capsules, granules, and oral liquids should not be used interchangeably.

Please note that these are translations of common Chinese pharmaceutical terms, not dosage recommendations for any specific medicine. The actual dosage and frequency should follow the relevant package insert or the guidance of a qualified healthcare professional. Consumers should not adjust the dosage or frequency on their own.

**2. Adverse Reactions**

This section describes possible adverse reactions associated with the use of a medicine.

Some traditional Chinese medicine package inserts list known adverse reactions, while others use the phrase "尚不明确," meaning that the adverse reactions are not yet clearly established.

This does not mean that the medicine has no adverse reactions. Rather, the available information remains unclear or insufficient.

If noticeable discomfort or unusual reactions occur after using a medicine, consumers should seek medical assistance promptly.

**3. Contraindications**

Contraindications are an important part of a package insert. This section generally explains circumstances in which a particular medicine should not be used.

Some traditional Chinese medicine products may have restrictions for pregnant women, breastfeeding women, or people with specific allergies.

If you are unsure whether any of these restrictions apply to you, consult a doctor or pharmacist before using the product.

**4. Precautions**

This section provides additional information about issues that consumers should be aware of during medication use.

It may include requirements for specific populations, dietary considerations, and potential risks associated with using the medicine alongside other medications.

If you are taking prescription medicines, over-the-counter (OTC) products, or other traditional Chinese medicines, inform your doctor or pharmacist so that potential drug interactions can be assessed.

**5. Storage**

Storage requirements may vary depending on the ingredients, dosage form, and packaging of a traditional Chinese medicine product.

Common storage instructions include:
- Keep tightly sealed.
- Protect from light.
- Store in a cool and dry place.
- Refrigerate.

For liquid formulations and specially packaged products, storage requirements after opening may differ from those for unopened products. Always follow the instructions provided in the specific package insert.

👉 Understanding these terms allows consumers to read package inserts more comprehensively rather than focusing only on the medicine name or the functions described on the packaging.

## Ⅲ. What Is the Difference Between Chinese Patent Medicines, Herbal Decoction Pieces, and Formula Granules?

Traditional Chinese medicine products are available in various forms in pharmacies across China. These products differ not only in appearance but also in their labeling requirements and methods of use.

Understanding these basic categories can help international consumers identify different types of traditional Chinese medicine products more accurately.

**1. Chinese Patent Medicines**

Chinese patent medicines are pharmaceutical products manufactured according to established formulas and production processes using traditional Chinese medicinal materials.

Common dosage forms include tablets, capsules, granules, and oral liquids.

These products generally come with standardized packaging and package inserts. Consumers should pay particular attention to the ingredients, functions and indications, directions and dosage, and contraindications.

**2. Chinese Herbal Decoction Pieces**

Chinese herbal decoction pieces are medicinal materials that have undergone processing procedures such as cleaning, cutting, and preparation.

They are commonly used in traditional Chinese medicine prescriptions and may appear in the form of slices, segments, or pieces.

When purchasing these products, consumers should check the herbal name, specifications, and processing information. The appropriate combination of herbs, dosage, and preparation methods should generally be determined according to a prescription from a qualified TCM practitioner or other professional guidance. Consumers should not combine herbs or adjust dosages on their own.

**3. Chinese Medicine Formula Granules**

Chinese medicine formula granules are produced using qualified Chinese herbal decoction pieces as raw materials. They undergo processing procedures such as extraction, concentration, and drying.

Their method of use differs from that of traditional herbal decoction pieces.

Consumers should follow the instructions for the specific product and professional guidance. Different formula granules should not be substituted for traditional herbal decoction pieces at equivalent dosages without professional advice.

Understanding these distinctions can help consumers identify different types of traditional Chinese medicine products in pharmacies and better understand their respective packaging and instructions.

## Ⅳ. What If You Cannot Understand Chinese Package Inserts? Practical Tips for International Consumers

For consumers who are unfamiliar with Chinese, language barriers can be one of the main challenges when purchasing medicines in China. However, several simple measures can make the purchasing process easier and help consumers obtain more accurate product information.

**1. Use Mobile Translation Tools**

Many mobile translation applications now support image recognition and camera-based translation. By taking photos of medicine packaging or package inserts, consumers can quickly obtain translations of medicine names, ingredients, and basic product information.

However, machine translation may not accurately interpret specialized traditional Chinese medicine terminology. In particular, information about dosage, contraindications, and potential drug interactions should not be understood solely through automated translation.

**2. Keep the Original Packaging and Package Insert**

After purchasing medicine, consumers are advised to retain the original packaging and package insert.

These materials make it easier to check the medicine name, ingredients, expiry date, and usage instructions when needed.

If you need to consult a doctor or pharmacist overseas, providing the complete original packaging and product information can also help them identify the medicine more accurately.

**3. Show the Medicine Name or a Photo to a Pharmacist**

If you are unsure how to communicate your needs in Chinese, prepare the medicine name, a product photo, or translated text in advance.

Explaining your actual needs, known allergies, and any other medications you are currently taking can help pharmacists provide more relevant information.

**4. Check the Medicine's Source and Packaging Information**

Whenever possible, purchase medicines through legitimate pharmacies and lawful pharmaceutical supply channels.

Check the integrity of the packaging, manufacturing date, expiry date, and relevant approval information.

For medicines that require special storage conditions, it is also important to confirm whether the transportation and storage conditions meet the product requirements.

**5. Check Cross-Border Medication Regulations in Advance**

If you plan to take traditional Chinese medicine purchased in China back to your home country, check the destination country's regulations on medicine imports, personal medication allowances, herbal materials, and relevant ingredients in advance.

Different countries and regions have different requirements for importing traditional Chinese medicine, particularly products containing animal-derived ingredients or regulated substances.

A medicine that can be legally purchased in China is not necessarily permitted to be carried into another country.

Therefore, consumers should check the relevant regulations before purchasing and retain the original packaging, package insert, and proof of purchase to help avoid potential issues when traveling internationally.
