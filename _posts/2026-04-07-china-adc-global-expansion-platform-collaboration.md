As a China-based pharmaceutical distributor serving global markets, DengYueMed has been closely tracking a notable industry shift: Chinese antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) are rapidly expanding into global markets, evolving from asset out-licensing toward technology-driven collaboration.

As of early 2026, Chinese biopharma companies have completed approximately 44 ADC-related business development (BD) transactions, with a disclosed total deal value exceeding $53.2 billion, including more than $3.1 billion in upfront payments.

More importantly, the structure of these transactions is evolving—from single-asset licensing to multi-asset partnerships and platform-level collaborations—signaling a transition from product export to technology-centric global engagement.

---

## I. From Asset Out-Licensing to Platform Collaboration: A Three-Phase Evolution

The globalization of China’s ADC sector has followed a clear three-phase trajectory.

### Phase 1 (2019–2021): Initial Entry via Late-Stage Asset Licensing

In 2021, RemeGen out-licensed global rights to its HER2-targeting ADC to Seagen in a deal valued at approximately $2.6 billion.

At this stage:
- Assets were near or post-approval  
- Clinical data were mature  
- Risk for partners was relatively low  

---

### Phase 2 (2022–2024): Expansion into Clinical-Stage Partnerships

Between 2022 and 2024, ADC collaborations expanded into earlier-stage assets.

A representative example is the Kelun-Biotech–Merck collaboration, covering multiple ADC programs with a total deal value exceeding $11 billion.

### Phase 3 (2025–Present): Platform-Level Collaboration

Since 2025, ADC globalization has entered a new phase:

- Platform licensing  
- Multi-asset co-development  
- Long-term strategic partnerships

## III. Key Challenges and Structural Risks

Despite strong growth, challenges remain:

- Target concentration  
- Milestone-heavy deal structures  
- Limited global commercialization capability  
- IP constraints

## Conclusion

China’s ADC expansion reflects a shift from cost-driven participation to innovation-driven leadership.

Future expansion is expected in:
